This is a hard read, and an important one. Jason Grigsby describes trying to cancel a tracking-collar subscription after his dog died, only to be taken through a retention flow that kept trying to sell him on keeping it.

What stays with me is how little room the interface made for his actual circumstances. None of the cancellation reasons acknowledged that a pet might have died, and each additional offer asked more of someone who was already grieving. We can let people leave without making them explain a loss or fend off another sales pitch. That’s worth considering when we review a cancellation flow, not only after someone writes to tell us how badly it hurt.
